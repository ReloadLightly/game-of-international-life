## Stable project identity

Game of International Life is a research and learning repository for cellular automata, artificial life, spatial agent-based modeling, and evolutionary rule discovery applied to international relations under anarchy.

The repository has two linked purposes:

1. learn cellular automata rigorously through canonical systems such as Conway's Game of Life;
2. construct explicit computational thought experiments for IR theories and compare the macro-patterns generated by their mechanisms under matched worlds.

## Interpretive boundary

M1 is a generative mechanism check, not an empirical validation of Jervis.

M2 is a geopolitical world substrate, not yet a test of Waltz or Mearsheimer. Its opportunistic attack rule contains substantive assumptions and is deliberately named as a baseline rather than a theory. Comparative claims begin only when rival policy rules operate inside the same M2 world with identical geography, resources, information, event resolution, and shocks.

## Decisions that should not be silently reversed

1. Conway's Life remains a faithful baseline rather than being relabeled as IR.
2. IR models use explicit theory-to-mechanism translations.
3. Geographic cell identity and political identity remain separate.
4. M2 world mechanics remain shared across rival theory rules.
5. Hand-coded baselines precede GA/GP rule evolution.
6. Matched seeds and ensemble trajectories take priority over attractive single animations.
7. Alliance relations will use a graph layer rather than being forced into geography.
8. Complexity is added only to answer a concrete research question.
9. Polity IDs are not recycled; extinction and succession remain traceable.
10. Fragmentation restores connected territorial polities after a bridge loss.

## Exact next milestone

**M3 — Structural worlds and the first matched rule comparison.**

The smallest meaningful M3 should preserve M2 and add a policy interface with two hand-coded rules:

1. **security-seeking sufficiency:** attack or expand only when doing so closes a clearly defined vulnerability or survival deficit;
2. **relative-power opportunity:** exploit favorable territorial opportunities even after immediate defensive sufficiency has been reached.

Before calling either rule “Waltzian” or “Mearsheimerian,” M3 should specify:

- the exact information visible to a polity;
- how system-level capability distribution enters decisions;
- which assumptions are shared and which differ;
- matched initial worlds and battle shocks;
- directional hypotheses and failure criteria;
- process observables capable of separating similar final outcomes.

A useful first experiment would cross initial capability concentration with resource geography and compare survival, concentration, preventive war, territorial turnover, and systemic expenditure under both rules.
