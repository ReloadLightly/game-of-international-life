# Research program

## Core question

Which macro-patterns of international order can be generated by local strategic interaction under anarchy, and which theoretical mechanisms produce distinguishable outcomes and process signatures?

## Why cellular and spatial agent-based models?

Cellular automata enforce a useful form of theoretical austerity. Information is local, time is explicit, update rules are inspectable, and no central planner is needed to produce system-level order. They are especially appropriate for questions involving borders, neighborhood threats, diffusion, cascades, path dependence, territorial concentration, and emergent actors.

They are less natural for long-distance alliances, global markets, institutions, and rich strategic cognition. Those features should be added as graph layers or actor state rather than forced into geographic adjacency.

M2 is deliberately a hybrid spatial model: geographic sites interact locally on a hex lattice, while multi-cell polities aggregate production and capability. This is necessary once a state is no longer identical to one cell.

## Research ladder

### Level A — Formal and software correctness

- verify synchronous updates;
- test canonical Conway patterns;
- test square and hex neighborhood semantics;
- enforce connected territorial polities;
- guarantee deterministic replay for a fixed seed;
- record all experiment parameters.

### Level B — Mechanism verification

- construct extreme cases whose direction is theoretically clear;
- confirm that distinguishability changes perceived threat without changing actual posture;
- confirm that offense advantage changes attack–defense incentives;
- confirm that resource control changes polity capability;
- confirm that overwhelming local advantage can produce conquest and extinction;
- confirm that severed territories fragment into successor states;
- inspect trajectories and event traces, not only end states;
- search for unintended computational shortcuts.

### Level C — Comparative theory experiments

- implement at least two rival policy-rule families inside the same world;
- share initial maps, resources, information, and exogenous shocks;
- preregister directional hypotheses and failure criteria;
- measure robustness across seeds and parameter regions;
- compare process signatures as well as final outcomes;
- identify observational equivalence and parameter-dependent reversals.

### Level D — Computational fossil-record replication

- reconstruct published spatial IR models as faithfully as surviving documentation allows;
- separate replication from modernization;
- reproduce qualitative and quantitative headline findings;
- compare original rules with modern alternatives.

### Level E — Evolutionary discovery

- evolve interpretable local rules or small programs;
- use hand-coded theories as baselines and ancestors;
- evaluate across distributions of worlds, not one map;
- hold out topologies, resource fields, and shocks;
- archive behavioral diversity, not only scalar fitness;
- translate discovered rules back into theoretical propositions.

### Level F — Empirical contact

- initialize geography and capabilities from documented data;
- estimate only parameters that have empirical meaning;
- use historical periods for calibration and separate periods for evaluation;
- compare process traces where possible, not only final distributions;
- report where the abstraction is too severe for empirical inference.

## Minimal experiment template

Every comparative experiment should eventually contain:

1. a precise research question;
2. theory-to-rule translation;
3. shared world assumptions and rule-family differences;
4. hypotheses and failure criteria;
5. initial-condition generator;
6. parameter file;
7. deterministic run command;
8. run-level and event-level data;
9. aggregation and sensitivity analysis;
10. plots generated from saved data;
11. interpretation that distinguishes result from speculation.

## Current substrate: M2 territorial states

M2 now implements:

- stable cells distinct from mutable polity identity;
- odd-row six-neighbor hex geometry;
- connected multi-cell territories;
- local resource production;
- polity-level treasury and capability aggregation;
- borders, attack, defense, conquest, and explicit costs;
- state extinction and connected-component fragmentation;
- reproducible artificial geographies;
- state-size, polarity, border, and war observables;
- generation-level ensemble trajectories.

The baseline attack selector is generic and opportunistic. It should not become the hidden default inside every future theory. The world and event-resolution layer should remain stable while policy selection becomes an explicit replaceable component.

## Immediate next milestone: M3 matched realist rules

The next smallest data-bearing experiment should add two policy rules to the same M2 substrate:

- **security-seeking sufficiency:** expansion is permitted only to reduce a specified vulnerability or restore a security margin;
- **relative-power opportunity:** favorable expansion remains valuable beyond immediate defensive sufficiency.

A first factorial experiment can vary:

- initial capability concentration;
- number of major powers;
- clustered versus dispersed resource geography;
- power-projection costs or frontier dilution;
- observability of rival capability.

Candidate discriminating observables include preventive attacks, attacks after sufficiency, territorial concentration, effective powers, state survival, war expenditure, border volatility, and the frequency with which rising powers trigger counter-expansion.

## Evolutionary-computation extension

The passage from Iba's book motivates a later transition from hand-designed rules to evolutionary rule search. The representation should remain interpretable. Candidate forms include:

- Boolean expressions over named local and systemic features;
- decision lists;
- small typed GP trees;
- finite-state transition tables;
- linear combinations of theory variables followed by thresholds.

Potential objectives should not merely reward “peace” or “power.” Interesting searches include:

- robust survival across unknown strategic worlds;
- low expenditure subject to a survival constraint;
- deterrence without uncontrolled conflict cascades;
- alliance policies balancing abandonment and entrapment;
- rules reproducing multiple empirical stylized facts simultaneously;
- novelty search for previously unseen but stable forms of order.

The most valuable output is not the winning score. It is a compact rule that survives held-out worlds and can be expressed as a new theoretical conjecture.
